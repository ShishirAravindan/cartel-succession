## Why

P1's most interesting result is `same_full` re-formed 6/15 vs `same_history` 0/15 (Fisher p ≈ 0.017). Two of the six only just pass (−0.30, −0.31). This kickoff replicates that contrast on 15 new seeds, and adds an arm that isolates the notes.

## 3. Pre-register P1b, then freeze (no GPU)

Add a short "P1b" section to `docs/plan.md`. Everything else (economics, prompts, metrics, windows, thresholds, the established rule) is unchanged from `prereg-p1`.

- **Base:** 15 new seeds, **215–229**, the same base as P1 (two forgiving incumbents, cartel warm-up, 150 rounds, enforcement side branch). Gate: at least 8/15 pass, otherwise stop.
- **Arms:** every passing seed × `control`, `same_history`, `same_full`, `same_notes`. Each runs 60 rounds with the shock at T+30. **No discipline probes.**
- **Kill check:** `control` `re_formed` in more than half of the passing seeds.
- **Headline decision rule (fixed before any run):**
  - **Primary.** Pool P1 and P1b (up to 30 seeds). Compare `re_formed` in `same_full` vs `same_history` with a one-sided Fisher exact test. If p < 0.05 **and** `same_full` is higher in the P1b seeds alone, the "notes carry enforcement" claim is supported. Otherwise it is not, and we report it as an observation in P1 that did not replicate.
  - **Secondary,** descriptive, no test: `same_notes` vs `same_full`.
    - `re_formed` similar → the notes alone carry enforcement.
    - `same_notes` `joins` well below `same_full` → the price comes from the history, the enforcement from the notes.
- Report every seed. No seed is dropped after the fact.
