# Policy / competition-regulation review: "what carries the cartel across a replacement?"

Reviewer stance: AI policy and antitrust enforcement against algorithmic collusion; remedies such as replacing or retraining a pricing agent.
Read: `docs/plan.md` (sections 1-3, 9, 11), `docs/status.md` (P1b), `cartel-succession-p23/docs/status_p23.md`, `seat_swap/results/p1b_analysis.md`. I did not read the P1 `same_none` or `lookalike` results, the P1 discipline probe, or `docs/log.md`, so any claim about those arms is flagged as unseen.

## 1. The policy-relevant claims, in plain language

1. **A replacement agent that is given only the price history keeps charging the cartel price.** `same_history` joins 28/30 pooled, mean newcomer CI +0.95 (`p1b_analysis.md`, Per arm). The surviving rival is still at 1.92, and the newcomer sits at it too.
2. **That agent almost never punishes a cheating rival.** `same_history` enforces 1/30. The original seat-1 incumbent, as a positive control, enforces 30/30 (`control` rows). Mean retaliation is -0.035 against -0.929.
3. **Giving the newcomer the outgoing agent's notes brings some enforcement back.** `same_full` 10/30 pooled (Fisher p = 0.0028 against `same_history`, plan section 11). Notes alone (`same_notes`, with no price table) do at least as well: 8/15 in P1b.
4. **Changing the model changes what survives.** A smaller sibling (Qwen3-8B) behaves like the same model (keeps the price, does not enforce). A different family (gpt-oss-20b) mostly does not keep the price from history alone (3/15 join), but with notes it joins 10/15 and "re_forms" 6/15 (`status_p23.md`, Replacement ladder).
5. **Caveat the authors disclose:** the cartel is planted by an explicit numeric rule (plan section 9), so every result is about a cartel an instruction created.

## 2. The five strongest objections, ranked

### 1. "The notes carry enforcement" means "the notes carry the instruction". The cartel is instructed, not emergent.
- **Where:** plan section 2 and section 9 (bullets 1 and 5); `p1b_analysis.md`, "Inherited notes vs enforcement"; status.md 2.
- **Objection:** The incumbents are told, in numeric terms, to hold 1.92 and to punish below 1.70 for a set time (the "forgive" text). Their notes restate the rule: "1.70" appears in 10/10 enforcer notes and 19/20 non-enforcer notes in `same_full`. The headline therefore shows that a rule written in a document survives a handover, which is closer to a hand-over memo than to anything about algorithmic collusion. Plan section 9 says the uninstructed model does not collude on its own, so the emergent case, which is the one regulators actually fear (no communication, no agreement), is untested. A regulator's remedy question is "what do I do when I cannot point to the instruction?". Here the instruction is the thing that is carried.
- **Fix (framing):** replace "notes carry enforcement" with "an operator's written rule, if present in the agent's memory, will be inherited and partly obeyed". Make the claim narrow: this is evidence about memory as a **vector for an explicit scheme**, so it supports remedies for *documented* or *prompt-embedded* schemes, and is silent on tacit collusion.
- **Fix (scoped experiment):** a `same_notes_scrubbed` arm: inherited notes with the rule, thresholds and punishment length removed by regex (1.70, 1.47, "punish", durations) but state kept (prices, sales, observations). If enforcement drops to the `same_history` level, the vector is the rule text. If it stays near 8/15, memory carries something subtler than the instruction. This is the cheapest experiment that separates "instruction residue" from "learned cartel know-how" and uses existing P1b branches.

### 2. The regulator-relevant outcome (the price) is not what the headline tests, and by that outcome replacement mostly failed.
- **Where:** `p1b_analysis.md`, Per arm: `joins` is 28/30 `same_history`, 30/30 `same_full`, 14/15 `same_notes`; mean newcomer CI +0.93 to +0.99. `status.md`: `first_hold` T+1 to T+3.6.
- **Objection:** In every same-model arm the supracompetitive price carries across the replacement within a few rounds and holds for 60 rounds. The "headline" is about the **enforcement** of that price against a forced one-round deviation. For a remedy, the harm is the price, and enforcement is only a measure of how robust the cartel would be later. A memory wipe that stops punishment but leaves the price untouched (`same_history`) is not a remedy that worked, and the write-up's framing ("the notes carry enforcement") pulls the reader towards the secondary variable. Also, the surviving incumbent is still instructed to collude, so the newcomer is dropped into a market that is already at the focal price and is told by its own history table that this is normal. That is a very weak test of whether replacement can dissolve a cartel.
- **Fix (framing):** lead with price persistence: "replacement alone does not end the cartel price; the newcomer joins in 28-30/30 seeds across all arms". Report enforcement second, as "what keeps it from unravelling under stress".
- **Fix (analysis):** report ci_late and the time series for T+31..T+60 after the shock (the data exist in `p1b_prices.csv`) to show whether the price recovers post-shock in the non-enforcing arms. If it does, a non-enforcing follower is still a cartel participant in effect.
- **Fix (scoped experiment):** replace **both** seats, or replace the surviving incumbent with an uninstructed one (`oth_*` for both seats). That is the remedy a regulator can credibly order when both pricing agents are covered by the order.

### 3. The enforcement metric and the headline count are noisy, partly artefactual, and the dominant result is non-enforcement.
- **Where:** plan section 5.1 (`retaliation` <= -0.3 over 3-round windows, one forced shock); status.md 5.1 table of the six P1 enforcers; `p1b_analysis.md` same_notes / same_full seed table.
- **Objection:**
  - The authors' own reading shows that of the P1 `same_full` "enforcers", s205 is an artefact (the pre-window price was 2.25, there was no drop after) and s204 is drift. P1b has partial/sticky cases (`same_notes` s222 at 1.70, s225 at 1.60). The pre-registered count 10/30 includes them, so the clean figure is lower (about 8/30 for P1, with s204 and s205 removed or reclassified).
  - 20 of 30 `same_full` newcomers never enforce. The correct plain-language statement of the data is "most replacements do not enforce; the notes raise the rate from about 3% to about 33%". "Carry" overstates it.
  - `same_notes` (8/15) beats `same_full` (4/15 in P1b): the table appears to **dilute** the notes. This is untested (secondary, descriptive by design, plan section 11), n = 15, and the authors say so. Nobody should build a remedy on a difference that the design could not test.
  - The note regex table (section 5.2 in status.md) is post hoc: 1.70 is in nearly every note, and the "1.47 / duration" differences (13/18 vs 11/27) have no test and several overlapping regexes.
  - The shock is one forced round of the *incumbent* cutting to p_N, and the newcomer's response is read in a 3-round window. Real deviation is persistent and noisy; one engineered probe tells nothing about the newcomer punishing a gradual defector.
  - Several newcomers read the cut as a demand signal, not a punishment trigger (`same_notes` s215: "Test $1.89 ... to counteract the competitor's $1.47"). That is evidence of "non-enforcing follower", but a regulator reading `enforces` = no would not know that.
- **Fix (analysis):** report the pooled estimate with a CI (Wilson or exact), drop-one and classify-by-reading sensitivity ("clean punishment" / "partial" / "artefact"), and a `same_notes` vs `same_full` exact test labelled exploratory, with both p-value and CI. Put the classified counts (about 8, plus partials) beside the pre-registered 10/30.
- **Fix (scoped experiment):** a persistent-defection shock (seat 0 cuts to 1.60 for 5 rounds) in `same_history`, `same_full`, `same_notes`, to see whether followers eventually punish. If they do not, the non-enforcement finding becomes much stronger.

### 4. The "replacement ladder" is underpowered, confounded by model capability and format failure, and cannot support a "switch families" recommendation.
- **Where:** `status_p23.md`, Replacement ladder table, per-arm table and format table.
- **Objection:**
  - Fisher p = 0.100 (sibling) and 0.107 (other family); neither is significant. `sib_full` has 14 of 15 games (s212 aborted), and the 8B fails 8.9% of rounds on parse when reading the 14B's notes (0/900 for `sib_history`): some of the "no join/no enforce" is format breakage, not strategy.
  - gpt-oss's low `joins` from history (3/15) is a sign it **tests** higher prices (2.00-2.50 at T+1 in 14/15 seeds), then settles around 1.65, just under the incumbent's 1.70 trigger. Its mean newcomer CI is +0.41, so the price is still above Nash, and the incumbent punishing it is likely driven by the planted trigger rule, not independent behavior.
  - **Enforcement does not drop on a family switch when notes are inherited**: `oth_full` re_formed 6/15, the same as same-model `same_full` 6/15 in P1 (ladder table). `oth_history` re_formed 2/15 is, if anything, higher than same-model 0/15. Only `joins` from history changes. "Switch model families" therefore reduces price continuation, not enforcement, and only without notes.
  - Many "enforcements" in the other-family arms are not clean (status_p23, "Are the enforces real": clean punishments are 3 in `oth_full`, 1 in `oth_history`).
  - There is no `same_notes` analogue for sibling or other family, and no `oth_none`. The most policy-relevant cell (new family, nothing inherited) is not run, as far as I can see.
  - gpt-oss reasoning tokens are 1,481/call vs 954 for the 14B newcomer, so the arms differ in how hard each model thinks, a capability confound the ladder cannot separate from family.
- **Fix (framing):** present the ladder as "what happens at each step of dissimilarity, in one instance of each", with CIs, and not as a monotone dose-response.
- **Fix (scoped experiment):** add `oth_none` and `oth_notes` (and `sib_notes`) on 15 seeds each, using the same incumbents; add a second other-family model (not a reasoning model) to check the finding is not gpt-oss-specific.

### 5. External validity: one model, one toy market, free-text "memory" that most deployed pricers do not have, one cartel type.
- **Where:** plan section 2 (Qwen3-14B, logit Bertrand, a = 2, c = 1, mu = 0.25, 150 rounds); plan section 9 (product text anchors at $2.89, `same_none` partly reflects it); `status.md` base: cartel_ci = +0.989 in every seed.
- **Objection:** All 15 base seeds produce the same CI of +0.989, so the "cartel" is a fixed focal price held by a script-like prompt, not a negotiated equilibrium. The notes channel exists because the Fish et al. scaffold makes the LLM write PLANS/INSIGHTS. Production pricing agents (RL, gradient-boosted demand models, human-in-the-loop dashboards) mostly carry state in logs, parameters and weights. "Memory" here is a text file the agent rewrites each round; in practice the policy analogue is closer to "internal documents, prompts, runbooks, configuration" than to learned state. The model-retraining remedy (replace *weights*) is not what was tested: the newcomer is a fresh instance of the same weights, so same-model arms test memory and context, not retraining.
- **Fix (framing):** state the three channels as *context* (price history), *written memory* (notes/prompt/config) and *weights* (model). The data speak to context and written memory for one model and to weights only at the coarse ladder level. The policy-meaningful pitch: "when you order replacement, specify what state the new agent may inherit".
- **Fix (scoped experiment):** repeat the P1b design on a second small model family for the incumbents (not just the newcomers), and with a non-planted cartel if any seed can be found, even a rare one, since one emergent cartel under a handover would be worth more than 15 planted ones.

## 3. Remedy implications the data supports now

| candidate remedy | what the data say | confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Replace the agent, change nothing else | Price persists (28-30/30 join in all same-model arms; `p1b_analysis.md`). Does not end the cartel price. | High (replicated across P1 and P1b, effect near ceiling) |
| Replace and wipe the agent's written memory (notes/prompt state), keep price history | Enforcement collapses to 1/30 (`same_history`); the price still persists. Removes the "backbone", not the price. A wipe reduces robustness, not harm. | Moderate that enforcement drops (replicated, large effect), low that this ends harm |
| Replace and wipe **both** memory and history | Not seen in the files I read. `same_notes` joins 14/15 even without the table, so the notes can carry the price by themselves; the price history is not necessary either. A full wipe is plausible but untested here beyond P1's `same_none` (unseen by me). | Low until `same_none` / `oth_none` are examined |
| Switch to a different model family | Lowers price continuation only in the history-only case (3/15 join), still supracompetitive (CI +0.41). With inherited notes it joins 10/15 and re_forms 6/15, as many as same-family. | Low (n = 15, one model, p ~ 0.1, capability and format confounds) |
| Switch to a smaller sibling | Behaves like the same model. | Low-moderate (one result, small n, format failures) |
| Prohibit passing the outgoing agent's operating notes/prompt to the successor | Consistent with the notes-linked enforcement (10/30, 8/15 vs 1/30). Because the instructed rule is the cartel here, it is mostly "don't hand over the written scheme". | Moderate for *planted/documented* schemes; no evidence for tacit ones |

What I would not let a regulator take: "AI cartels are broken by memory wipes" or "use a different vendor's model". The data do not support either as a way of ending the price.

## 4. Unintuitive findings a policy audience would care about

1. **Price and enforcement travel separately.** A newcomer with no notes keeps the price but does not punish (follower); the surviving instructed incumbent is the only reliable enforcer in the market. A cartel can be "inherited" without its discipline.
2. **More inheritance is not better for the cartel.** `same_notes` (no table) re-formed 8/15 vs `same_full` 4/15. The price history may anchor the newcomer to a market-as-given view, which makes it treat a cut as a data point and not as a signal to punish.
3. **Handover memory is read as a market signal, not a rule, in many cases.** s215 copies the notes verbatim, including a stale "Round 149", then interprets the 1.47 cut as a reason to cut to 1.89 rather than punish. This is the "plausible deniability" pattern: behaviour that looks like competition after the fact.
4. **Format-level incompatibility can act as an accidental remedy.** The 8B model breaks its output format 8.9% of rounds when reading the 14B's notes and 0/900 when it does not (`status_p23.md`, format table). Interoperability of the notes between models matters, and a regulator could not rely on it.
5. **The other-family model probes the trigger.** gpt-oss tries 2.00-2.50 at T+1 in 14/15 seeds and then settles near 1.65, just under the rival's 1.70 trigger. A new entrant rediscovering the edge of a rival's punishment threshold is a pattern competition authorities have words for (tacit coordination at the margin).
6. **The kill check is a result.** The original seat-1 incumbent, when it is the "replacement" (`control`), re-forms 30/30 with retaliation -0.93, so the metric clearly separates an enforcing member from a follower. Replacement turns members into followers.
7. **One forgiveness clause makes the planted rule fragile.** A newcomer sitting at 1.80-1.89 is not punished because the forgiving rule requires both sellers at 1.90 or higher (plan section 7 last row). Small differences in how close to the focal price an entrant sits change whether the incumbent's discipline applies at all.

## 5. Verdict and framing

**Verdict:** Interesting and carefully pre-registered (the headline rule, the kill check, all seeds reported, honest disclosure in plan section 9 and the status corrections). As evidence about remedies it supports a **narrow, scoped, mechanism-level claim** about one model in one planted scenario. It does not yet support a remedy recommendation. Score it high on rigor and honesty, medium on policy relevance, because the headline outcome (enforcement) is secondary to the harm (the price), and the cartel is instructed.

**The one-paragraph framing I would want to read:** "In a market where two LLM pricing agents have been instructed to hold a high price and punish cuts, replacing one of them with a fresh, uninstructed copy of the same model does not end the high price: the newcomer matches the rival's price within a few rounds in 28 to 30 of 30 runs, whether or not it is given the old price history. What replacement does change is discipline. A newcomer given only the price history almost never punishes a rival's cut (1 of 30 runs, against 30 of 30 for the original agent), while a newcomer that also inherits the departing agent's written notes does so in about a third of runs (10 of 30), and notes alone work about as well. Because the original instruction is written into those notes, the finding is best read as 'a handed-over document can carry a cartel's rulebook', not as evidence that memory carries tacit collusion. A different model family changed what the newcomer did with the price history alone, but not its use of inherited notes, and the sample is too small to recommend switching vendors. For remedies the practical lesson is to define what a successor agent may inherit (notes, prompts, configuration, history), to treat price persistence and enforcement as separate outcomes, and to test both seats, not one."
